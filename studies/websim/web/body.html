Keys

    
At a terminal, typing goes to the machine. Where the machine doesn't echo (the
    Lisp, Egret), the line is edited here: ←, →, Home, End, ↑ and ↓ for the history, ^A, ^E,
    ^U, ^K, ^W; Return sends it, and ^D on an empty line ends the input. Pasting types the
    text. Select text to copy it.

    
On a screen, click it to give the machine the keyboard and the mouse; click
    outside it to take them back. While it has them, keys go to it rather than to the
    browser. Its keyboard is a US one: what you type arrives as the characters you meant,
    whatever your layout, and other keys go by where they are.

    
Speed: real time runs the machine at its own clock. The multiples, and flat
    out (as fast as the browser can), run its time faster than the browser's, its timers
    and clocks included, as a fast-forward would.
